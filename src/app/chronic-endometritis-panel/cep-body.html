Clinical indication

Who this panel is for

Chronic endometritis is a low-grade, often asymptomatic inflammation of the uterine lining that standard workups routinely miss — because conventional culture misses intracellular and slow-growing organisms, and histology alone can't tell you which organism is responsible. Order this panel when the clinical picture points to an infective cause behind reproductive failure.

  • 01
    Recurrent implantation failureRepeated unsuccessful embryo transfers with no other identified cause
  • 02
    Recurrent pregnancy lossTwo or more consecutive miscarriages
  • 03
    Unexplained infertility before IVFAs part of the pre-IVF work-up, once other causes are excluded
  • 04
    Abnormal uterine bleedingIrregular or intermenstrual bleeding without a structural cause
  • 05
    Hysteroscopic findingsMicropolyps, hyperaemia, or stromal oedema seen on hysteroscopy
  • 06
    Suspected genital tuberculosisWhere endometrial TB is on the differential for infertility

Why molecular detection

Culture vs. molecular detection

Conventional culture has been the default for decades — but it was never built to catch organisms like Mycoplasma or Ureaplasma, which don't grow well outside the body.

Sample requirements

Specimen collection

Four accepted specimen types, each with its own handling requirement.

Biopsy (formalin)

10% neutral buffered formalin, 10:1 ratio, room temperature.

Biopsy (sterile container)

In normal saline, DNase-free. 2–8°C, to lab within 24 hours.

Menstrual blood

Sterile cup, collected in the first 12 hours of menses. 2–8°C.

Aspirate / endometrial wash

Sterile container. 2–8°C.

FAQ

Common questions

Each of the three steps answers a different question. The biopsy provides the tissue. CD138 IHC confirms that plasma cells are present — the histological hallmark of chronic endometritis. The DICE PCR panel identifies the specific organism driving the inflammation, which is what determines antibiotic choice. Skip any one and you're left with an incomplete answer: a positive IHC with no organism identified, or an organism identified with no histological confirmation.
The two arms need incompatible preservation methods. Formalin fixes tissue for histology and immunohistochemistry, but it also destroys DNA — so it can't be used for the PCR arm. The molecular sample is instead collected in a sterile, DNase-free container in normal saline and kept refrigerated. Splitting the biopsy at collection is what lets one procedure serve both tests.
MTB Complex indicates genital tuberculosis and supports starting anti-TB therapy. NTM (non-tuberculous mycobacteria) is a different group of organisms entirely — it is not tuberculosis, and does not warrant ATT. Because the two can be confused on less specific assays, this panel reports them independently so a positive NTM result doesn't lead to unnecessary anti-TB treatment.
The endometrial biopsy is a short outpatient procedure, typically completed in under 15 minutes. Mild cramping similar to menstrual cramps is common; most patients resume normal activity the same day.
The DICE PCR Panel alone reports in 4 days. The Comprehensive panel, which adds histopathology and CD138 IHC read alongside the molecular result, reports in 6 days.
Please include relevant clinical history — indication for testing, hysteroscopic findings if available, and any recent antibiotic use, since antibiotics can lower bacterial DNA levels in the sample and affect detection.
